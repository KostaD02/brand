# Scroll progress

A thin bar pinned across the top of the viewport that fills as the page scrolls. It is
driven entirely by CSS: `animation-timeline` reads the scroller directly, so there is no
scroll handler, no resize listener and no work on the main thread.

## Example

The real component is `position: fixed` against the viewport at `--kd-z-toast`, so the
demos on this page are boxed. Each one wraps the bar in a `position: relative` frame,
switches it to `position: absolute`, and points `--kd-scroll-progress-timeline` at a
`scroll-timeline-name` on the inner scroller, so the bar measures that box instead of the
window.

## What it measures

Out of the box the timeline is `scroll(root block)`, the document scroller, and the range is
`normal`: empty at the top of the page, full at the very bottom. That is the right default
for a whole-page indicator and needs no markup beyond the element itself.

```html
<div class="kd-scroll-progress" aria-hidden="true"></div>
```

> Give it `aria-hidden="true"`. Scroll position is already conveyed by the scrollbar and by
> the act of scrolling, so announcing it adds nothing, and the element holds no text to
> announce in the first place.

## Tracking one element

A whole-page bar reaches full only at the bottom of the footer. For a reading indicator you
usually want the article instead, which is what `kd-scroll-progress--subject` is for: mark
the element with `kd-scroll-subject` and the bar switches to that element's view timeline,
over the range where it covers the scrollport.

```html
<div class="kd-scroll-progress kd-scroll-progress--subject" aria-hidden="true"></div>
<article class="kd-scroll-subject">...</article>
```

The bar sits empty until the subject's top edge reaches the top of the scrollport, fills
across it, and holds full once its bottom edge has passed.

<Canvas of={ScrollProgressStories.Subject} />

> A named timeline only resolves on the element itself and its ancestors, and the bar is
> almost never a descendant of what it measures. The reset entry declares
> `timeline-scope: --kd-scroll-subject` on `body` so the two can find each other anywhere in
> the document. The cost is that the name has to be unique: give two elements
> `kd-scroll-subject` on one page and the timeline becomes ambiguous, which leaves the bar
> collapsed rather than tracking either of them.

## Browser support

Scroll-driven animations are Baseline newly available: Chrome and Edge from 115, Safari from
26, Firefox from 159. The bar is deliberately `scale: 0 1` at rest, so where
`animation-timeline` is not understood the declaration is dropped, the animation never runs,
and the element stays collapsed. Older browsers get no bar rather than a broken one, which is
why no `@supports` guard is needed.

## Orientation

`kd-scroll-progress--vertical` turns the bar into a column down the inline-start edge that
fills top to bottom. The timeline is unchanged, so the two orientations read the same
progress and the modifier composes with `--subject` exactly as the default does.

```html
<div class="kd-scroll-progress kd-scroll-progress--vertical" aria-hidden="true"></div>
```

<Canvas of={ScrollProgressStories.Vertical} />

> The bar grows along the block axis in both writing directions, so only the edge it sits on
> flips under `dir="rtl"`, never the fill. To pin it to the opposite edge instead, set
> `inset-inline: auto 0` on the element.

## Direction

The default bar fills from the right edge towards the left under `dir="rtl"`, and the
vertical one moves to the opposite edge while still filling downward. Nothing to set: switch
the Direction control in the toolbar above and the demos follow.

## Reduced motion

The reset entry clamps `animation-duration` to `0.01ms` for everything under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. That clamp is meant for time-driven animation and makes no
sense for a bar whose progress is the reader's own scrolling, so the entry restores
`animation-duration: auto` for this block. Chromium ignores the clamp on a scroll-driven
animation either way; the override is there so a stricter engine cannot collapse the whole
range into the first scrolled pixel.

## Alongside a drawer

Opening `kd-drawer` locks the page, so the bar would otherwise sit frozen over the overlay
and read as a stray accent line. The reset entry fades it out for as long as a drawer is in
the document, through the same `--kd-duration-base` transition, and brings it back on close. No
JavaScript and nothing to wire up: it keys off the drawer element being present.

## Customising

Five properties are declared on the block. `--kd-scroll-progress-thickness` is the measure
across the bar, its height when horizontal and its width when vertical, and
`--kd-scroll-progress-bg` the fill, which defaults to `--kd-accent`.
`--kd-scroll-progress-inset` is the offset from the edge the bar is pinned to, for clearing
something already docked there.

<Canvas of={ScrollProgressStories.Customising} />

The remaining two are the wiring: `--kd-scroll-progress-timeline` is the timeline the bar
reads and `--kd-scroll-progress-range` the slice of it to fill across. Setting them by hand
covers anything the `--subject` modifier does not, such as a specific inner scroller or a
range that stops short of the end.

> Override the two together and set them _after_ `animation`, never through the shorthand.
> The `animation` shorthand resets `animation-timeline` and `animation-range` to their
> initial values, so a later shorthand silently drops the timeline and the bar stops moving.

> On desktop the bar spans the layout viewport, which excludes a classic scrollbar, so it
> stops a scrollbar's width short of the window edge at 100%. That gap is the scrollbar
> gutter and no fixed element can paint over it. Mobile and overlay scrollbars show no gap.
